# Chayei Sarah · Torah Or

We study the opening of “Inyan sedeh haMachpelah” in Torah Or. The Alter Rebbe connects the doubling of the letter he with eating, weekday birur and the vitality drawn down on Shabbat.

### עברית
יגלה לן טעמיה כו'. הנה מילוי ה' היא בה' אחרת והיא בחי' כפילא כנודע מענין מערת המכפלה שהיא ה' אחרונה דשם ב"ן כו'. והענין יובן בהקדים להבין ענין האכילה


### Translation
“May He reveal its meaning to us…” The letter he is spelled in full with another he. This is the aspect of doubling, known from the meaning of the Cave of Machpelah, which is the final he of the Name Ban. This will be understood by first considering the meaning of eating.


### Explanation
The Alter Rebbe opens with milui: spelling out a letter’s name with its component letters. Here he is written with another he; this doubling introduces the inner meaning of Machpelah. The discourse develops this through eating and Shabbat; its conclusion has not yet been reached.

## 2. Food is elevated through avodah

### עברית
כי הנה עיקר האכילה להיות כי מן המאכל נעשה חלב ודם והדם הוא הנפש וחיות האדם שמקבל הכבד הדם מן המאכל ושולח ללב ולמוח דתלת שליטין אינון בעלמא כבדא ולבא ומוחא כו'. ועי"ז מתעלה המאכל שנעשה ממנו לב ומוח האדם ועי"ז מתחזק לבו ומוחו בעבודת ה' שבלבו בהתלהבו' כרשפי אש שנעשה בכח האכילה שבלא אכילה הי' מתחלש כו'.


### Translation
The principal function of eating is that food becomes fat and blood; blood is the person’s life and vitality. The liver receives the blood from food and sends it to the heart and brain, for “three rule in the world: the liver, the heart and the brain.” Food is thereby elevated, becoming the human heart and brain and strengthening them for the service of Hashem: the heart burns like flames of fire through the strength received from eating; without food it would weaken.


### Explanation
The discourse follows food into the faculties through which a person serves Hashem. When its strength sustains the heart’s burning love, the food’s vitality participates in avodah and is elevated. This elevation prepares the explanation of birur in the next lesson.

## 3. From weekday birur to Shabbat delight

### עברית
וגם מוחו נעשה חזק להתבונן בגדולת ה' כו' וזהו ענין בירור שמן המאכל נעשה אדם. אך כ"ז הוא בימות החול אבל בשבת בורר אסור אלא האכילה היא יותר עליונה בבחי' המשכה מלמעלה וקראת לשבת ענג. אז תתענג על הוי"ה.


### Translation
The mind is also strengthened to contemplate the greatness of Hashem. This is the meaning of birur: food becomes a person. All this applies to weekdays; on Shabbat, however, selecting is forbidden, and eating belongs to a higher level: a drawing down from above. “You shall call Shabbat a delight”; “then you shall delight above Hashem.”


### Explanation
Birur here means the refinement through which food’s strength enters divine service. The Alter Rebbe distinguishes that weekday ascent from Shabbat’s hamshachah, an influence drawn down from above. The phrase “above Hashem” introduces the ensuing Kabbalistic explanation of higher delight.

## 4. The root of food in Tohu

### עברית
כי הנה כתיב אחור וקדם צרתני אחור למע"ב כו' שהמאכל כמו שהוא למטה בירידתו בשבה"כ הנה האדם הוא בבחי' קדם שהמאכל הוא מתברר ע"י האדם. אבל בשרשו למעלה הוא מעולם התהו לפני מלך מלך לבנ"י אלא שאח"כ וימלך וימת. וימת וימלך כו'. אך מ"מ נראה ששרשו יותר גבוה מהאדם שהרי המאכל הוא המחי' את האדם.


### Translation
It is written, “Behind and before You formed me”: behind in the work of Creation. In relation to food as it exists below, following its descent in the shattering of the vessels, the person is “before,” since the person refines the food. Yet in its higher root, food comes from the world of Tohu, “before a king reigned over the children of Israel.” Afterwards came “he reigned and died,” “he died and reigned.” Nevertheless, its root is seen to be higher than the person’s, for food is what gives the person life.


### Explanation
The discourse distinguishes food’s position after the shevirah from its earlier root in Tohu. Below, a person can refine it; at its root, food contains vitality from a higher level and can therefore sustain the person. “Before” and “behind” express these two relationships.

## 5. Shabbat: receiving vitality from the root

### עברית
והאדם הוא מקבל החיות מן המאכל לפי שהוא בבחי' אחור למע"ב כו' ובשבת שהוא זמן עליית העולמות מתעלה המאכל כמו שהוא בשרשו ונמשך ממנו חיות להנפש מלמעלה למטה ע"י י"ב לחמים כו' וזהו ענין צדיק אוכל לשובע נפשו כי יש ב' אכילות הא' הוא לשובע נפשו. והב' הוא ובטן רשעים תחסר. לשובע נפשו הוא חיות הנפש הבאה מלמעלה.


### Translation
The person receives vitality from food because, in this respect, the person is “behind” in the work of Creation. On Shabbat, the time of the worlds’ ascent, food rises as it exists in its root, and vitality is drawn from it into the soul from above to below through the twelve loaves. This is the meaning of “the righteous person eats to satisfy his soul.” There are two forms of eating: one is “to satisfy his soul,” and the other is “the belly of the wicked shall lack.” “To satisfy his soul” is the soul’s vitality that comes from above.


### Explanation
The ascent of the worlds on Shabbat enables the vitality of food to be received from its higher root. The discourse connects this hamshachah with the twelve loaves and “satisfying his soul.” It introduces a contrast with the other form of eating; that development is reserved for the next installment, without treating the discourse as finished.
